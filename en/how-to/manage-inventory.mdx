Operating without knowing what is on your shelves is the number one cause of lost margin in a Mexican small business. You sell what you no longer have, you notice shrinkage when it is already too late, and reports come out false because real stock does not match the system. El Nido gives you four tools to avoid it.

## 1. Recipes: each product deducts its ingredients

A burger is not "1 burger". It is:
- 1 brioche bun
- 150g ground beef
- 1 slice of yellow cheese
- 2 lettuce leaves
- 1 tomato (sliced)
- 30g French fries
- 1 takeaway box

You configure that recipe once. Each burger sale deducts 7 ingredients from stock automatically. At end of day you know you sold 47 burgers and your bun inventory dropped by 47 pieces (if everything reconciles).

## 2. Automatic low-stock alerts

For each ingredient you configure a minimum threshold. When stock hits the limit, the system alerts you.

Example:
- Brioche bun: alert when 20 pieces remain.
- Yellow cheese: alert when 3 packs remain.
- Ground beef: alert when 2kg remain.

Alerts arrive at the owner's app or a configurable WhatsApp channel. You reorder before hitting zero.

## 3. Counts from your phone

Each week (or as configured) you do a physical count of real inventory. With El Nido you do it from your phone:

1. Open Inventory → Count.
2. Scan barcodes or select ingredients.
3. Enter the real count (example: "I have 12 packs of cheese").
4. The system shows you the difference vs what should be there.
5. You adjust and the difference logs as shrinkage, adjustment or discrepancy.

## 4. Shrinkage logging

When an ingredient goes to waste (expired, damaged, given away), you log it as shrinkage with a reason:

| Reason | What it is for |
|---|---|
| **Expired** | Perishable past its date |
| **Damaged / spoiled** | Broke, fell, got contaminated |
| **Courtesy / gift** | You gave something to a customer or employee |
| **Theft / loss** | You do not know where it went (rare but happens) |
| **Sample / tasting** | For promotions or staff |

At month close you see how much each shrinkage category cost. The ones bleeding the most are the ones you address first.

## Nested recipes for prep items

If your business prepares something intermediate before selling (sauce, dough, mix), you can build a nested recipe:

- "House sauce" (1 liter) = 5kg tomato + 200g onion + 50g garlic + spices.
- "BBQ burger" uses "100ml house sauce" as one of its ingredients.

Subproduct cost and deduction calculate automatically.

## When you do NOT need this much control

- You sell finished products you buy and resell without preparing.
- You have no significant shrinkage.
- Your inventory is small and you count it mentally.

For direct resale without transformation (convenience store), counts and alerts are enough. Recipes are for businesses that prepare something.
