A bakery runs differently from a cafe or a restaurant. Most of the day's sales happen in the morning, the products are perishable (whatever doesn't sell today becomes waste), a lot of items sell by weight or by piece, and you restock batch after batch all day long.

## What your POS has to do if you sell baked goods

| Feature | Why it matters |
|---|---|
| **Fast tap sales** | At peak you're ringing up a ticket every 30 seconds |
| **Sell by weight** | Enter the weight at checkout and the POS calculates the total |
| **Daily waste control** | Record what went in the trash and understand the real cost |
| **Restock by batch** | Flag when a fresh batch comes out of the oven, no fuss |
| **Product of the day / out of stock** | Hide products when they run out without editing them |
| **Ticket with no printer if you want** | Send it by WhatsApp for orders over $100 |

## How a typical bakery uses El Nido

Saturday, 7 AM:

- A customer asks for 6 conchas, 4 bolillos, and 1 cake to go.
- The cashier taps the products on a visual grid (no digging through a menu).
- Automatic total, paid by card or cash in 15 seconds.
- At 10 AM a fresh batch of cuernitos comes out. The cashier marks "in stock" without editing anything.
- At 2 PM the day's bolillos run out. The product hides itself or you send it to "out of stock" until tomorrow.

## Waste control that actually matters

What goes in the trash is lost margin. With El Nido you record:

- Product left over today.
- Product that spoiled before it sold.
- Product given away at the end of the day.

At the end of the month you see: you sold $30,000 but waste came to $2,800. Now you adjust tomorrow's production so waste comes down.

## Products by weight (if that applies)

For bakeries that sell:

- Loose bread (concha by weight vs by piece)
- Cheese and cold cuts by the kilo
- Pasta and flour

You enter the weight or the quantity at checkout and the POS calculates the total at the price per kilo you configured.

## Points card for bakeries

Bakery customers are highly repeat (the same person buys every Saturday). With a digital card in Apple Wallet and Google Pay, they collect points with no physical card and redeem them for free bread or a discount on a birthday cake.

Real case: Churrería Las Duyas (León) runs on a digital points card and reports customers who no longer switch to nearby options because they have points saved up.

## When you don't need this

- You sell fewer than 50 pieces a day.
- You have no employees (you sell yourself).
- You don't care about measuring your waste.
- Nobody ever asks for an invoice.

At that size, a cash register and a notebook will do.
