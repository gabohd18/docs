A cafe sells things that are more complex than they look. A latte isn't one product, it's fifteen: small, medium, or large, whole, light, almond, or oat milk, with or without sugar, hot or iced, with an extra shot, decaf. If your POS forces you to create fifteen separate products to cover that, ringing up a drink takes longer than making it.

## What your POS has to do if you sell coffee

| Feature | Why it matters |
|---|---|
| **Variants** | Small, medium, and large latte from a single base product |
| **Modifiers** | Milk type, sugar, and extra shots added as toppings |
| **Built-in tips** | The customer picks 10%, 15%, or 20% on screen, no awkward moment |
| **Barista kitchen display** | The order lands straight on the bar screen, no paper slips |
| **To-go vs dine-in** | Tell takeout tickets apart from the ones staying in |
| **Digital points card** | Give regulars a reason to come back more often |

## How a typical cafe uses El Nido

Morning rush:

- The cashier builds the ticket: large latte, almond milk, no sugar, extra shot. Adds a 15% tip. Charges to card.
- The order shows up automatically on the barista's screen.
- The customer gets their ticket by WhatsApp or printed.
- If they're a regular, points stack up in their Apple Wallet without the cashier doing anything extra.

## Recipes with real cost

Every drink deducts what it uses:

- Latte = 7g of coffee + 200ml of milk + 1 paper cup + 1 lid + 1 straw = a real peso cost per drink.

With that number you know the margin on each product without guessing. If almond milk adds $8 to your cost, you decide whether to absorb it or charge extra.

## Digital points card for cafes

Your regulars (the ones who come by 3 to 5 times a week) collect points in Apple Wallet and Google Pay. No paper card that wears out. No app to install. Set it up as redeemable points, stamps (buy 10, get 1 free), or tiers.

Cafes with a loyal following tell us their customers stop switching to nearby competitors because they don't want to walk away from the points they've already earned.

## When you don't need this

- You sell fewer than 20 coffees a day and it's just you.
- You don't care which product leaves the most margin.
- You don't want to reward regulars.
- You take everything in cash and nobody asks for an invoice.

At that size, a tablet and a spreadsheet will do. A POS starts paying off once you have a barista, multiple shifts, frequent modifiers, or regular customers.
