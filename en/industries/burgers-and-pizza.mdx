## Combos as their own thing, not a sum

Many POS systems force you to create "combo 1" as a product separate from its parts. That breaks two things: if you change the burger price, the combo doesn't update; and if you want to know how many burgers you sold, it doesn't count the ones inside combos.

El Nido treats the combo as its own entity with selectable slots. When the customer builds it, the POS deducts inventory for the burger, fries and drink, but charges the combo price. And your reports show real sales by product.

## Measure your margin by combo

You log your **expenses** and ingredients (by hand or from a photo of the receipt) and El Nido shows you your **live income statement**: what each combo costs you, your waste, and the profit left over. That's how you find out whether your best-selling combo is also your most profitable one.

## When you don't need a POS for your burger spot

- You sell 10 burgers a day out of your garage.
- You have no employees.
- You don't care about measuring margin by combo.

At that size, a notebook works. A POS starts to make sense when you have volume, frequent combos, or growth toward several locations.
