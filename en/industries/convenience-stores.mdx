A Mexican neighborhood store (tiendita or abarrotes) has very specific pains a generic POS does not solve well. Lots of low-margin small items, store-credit sales known locally as "fiado", several suppliers with different payment terms and shrinkage that eats your margin if you do not track it.

## What your POS needs to do if you sell in a convenience store

| Feature | Why it matters |
|---|---|
| **Barcodes** | Ring up 60 products a day without searching each one for 10 seconds |
| **Store credit (fiado)** | Track "I'll write it down, pay me Saturday" without a parallel notebook |
| **Multi-payment per ticket** | Customer pays half in cash, half in transfer or voucher |
| **Low-stock alerts** | System tells you when 2 cans of tuna are left, not when shelves are empty |
| **Shrinkage control** | Expired, spoiled or stolen product recorded without friction |
| **Supplier reports** | Know who you spend the most with and which supplier is worth it |

## How a typical convenience store uses El Nido

- Morning: scan barcodes with the phone camera to check in new merchandise.

## When you do NOT need a POS for your store

- You sell fewer than 30 tickets a day and your notebook works.
- You have no employees (you and one family member).
- You do not care which product brings the most margin.
- Your suppliers do not extend credit and everything is cash.

For that size, a notebook and calculator still work. A POS earns its place when there is volume, employees ringing sales, constant store credit or growth.
